SAE J1939 is the protocol family used on trucks, coaches and other
commercial vehicles. It shares the physical CAN bus with passenger-car work
but not its addressing or its transport: J1939 identifies messages by PGN
and source address, and carries payloads over eight bytes with its own
TP.CM (connection management) and TP.DT (data transfer) frames.

## What the corpus shows

Five full-depth entries anchor the theme:

- [Expanding the attack scenarios of SAE J1939](../expanding-the-attack-scenarios-of-sae-j1939-a-comprehensive-analysis-of-established-and-novel-vulnerabilities-in-transport-protocol.md)
  (escar USA 2024) develops **14 scenarios** — seven from prior work and
  seven new — against the Request mechanism (PGN 59904) and the transport
  protocol. They include request overload, false Ack/Nack, competing
  RTS, connection exhaustion, BAM blocking, three out-of-range CTS memory
  reads, teardown/abort interruption, buffer exhaustion, TP.DT overwrite and
  data-frame spoofing. **11 of 14 executed successfully** in the authors'
  simulator testbed.
- [TruckDevil](../truckdevil.md) is the runnable heavy-vehicle assessment
  framework: PGN-aware discovery, message send/receive, a J1939 fuzzer and a
  plugin system, built on python-can.
- [pretty-j1939](../pretty-j1939.md) converts the SAE Digital Annex to a
  database and decodes candump logs into named PGNs/SPNs, reassembling BAM
  and RTS/CTS.
- [CANarchy](../canarchy.md) and [pycangui](../pycangui.md) cover J1939
  decoding inside broader CAN tooling.

## The synthesis

No entity page states the general claim: **J1939 is not CAN with different
IDs; its own request/acknowledge and multi-frame semantics create attacks
that generic CAN fuzzing and IDS rules do not model.**

The paper's most transferable observation is that **some attacks inject only
a single message**. A competing RTS or a false Ack changes the receiving
ECU's state without a flood, so rate-based detection has nothing to see.
Connection exhaustion and out-of-range CTS requests are similarly matters of
protocol state rather than bandwidth. A CAN IDS tuned to passenger-car
arbitration IDs and message rates does not describe these events.

The toolchain mirrors the split. Generic frame injection is insufficient:
TruckDevil is built around PGN semantics, and pretty-j1939 needs the SAE
Digital Annex to name what it sees. The blast radius is also different — a
compromised heavy vehicle is a fleet-scale safety problem, which is why the
KB's [Fleet Cyber Challenge](../fleet-cyber-challenge.md) treats the domain
as a programme rather than a single CTF.

## Counter-cases and limits

These are simulator testbed demonstrations, not attacks on a truck in
service. Three of the fourteen scenarios did not produce the intended impact
in the authors' setup, and the paper scopes itself to Request and TP, not
every J1939 application profile. The attacker model assumes bus access has
already been obtained. The defensive counter-case is the same as on
passenger cars: segmentation and gateway policy bound who reaches the bus,
but do not authenticate frames once an attacker is on it. No simulator,
vehicle or bus was exercised for this page.
