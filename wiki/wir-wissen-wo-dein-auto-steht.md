Kreil and Flupke, 38C3 2024. German-language talk on movement data of 800,000 Volkswagen group electric cars that was exposed unprotected online.

A 38C3 (2024) talk by Michael Kreil and Flüpke, in German, presenting the "Volksdaten" investigation (reported with Der Spiegel). Movement data for some 800,000 electric cars from across the Volkswagen Group — VW, Audi, SEAT, and Škoda — together with owner contact information, was stored unprotected and publicly accessible online: precise location and movement histories detailed enough to show who parks at home when, and who visits government and intelligence-agency sites or a brothel. The talk works through what the group collects and why, what the exposed data reveals about authorities, ministries, delivery and rental fleets as well as private owners, and how the team handled the data more carefully than the company that collected it.

What you get: the recorded talk on media.ccc.de; the recording carries multiple audio tracks (including translation) and subtitles. It earns its place in an English-language list as the definitive public account of this exposure — the automotive industry's largest connected-car data-protection failure to date. Caveats: this is a data-protection and investigative-journalism talk, not a vehicle-exploitation talk — nobody broke into a car; the failure was in how the backend stored fleet telemetry, and the talk is in German.
