Generic ISM band receiver for decoding TPMS sensors and key fobs at 315/433/868/915 MHz with RTL-SDR.

## What it is

A portable C program (GPL-2.0) that turns a cheap RTL-SDR dongle — the RTL2832U-based DVB-T receiver — into a data receiver for the licence-free ISM/SRD bands. (Despite the name it is a generic receiver, mainly for 433.92, 868, 315, 345, and 915 MHz.) It listens, recognises transmissions from a large built-in library of device protocols, and prints structured decodes (JSON, CSV, MQTT, and other outputs for logging). It is a receiver only: the documented option set covers tuning, demodulation, analysis, and output — no transmit, replay, or signal generation.

## How it works

An RTL-SDR (or another front end via SoapySDR — the README names LimeSDR, PlutoSDR, and HackRF One as actively tested) tunes a band and rtl_433 demodulates and runs its decoder stack against the traffic. The README's supported-protocol list runs to roughly 380 decoders. Automotive coverage is a by-product of that breadth: about 40 of the decoders are TPMS (Steelmate, Schrader, Toyota, Ford, BMW, Jeep, Honda, and more), so wheel-sensor pressure/temperature/ID frames decode like any weather station, and around 28 more cover car remotes and keyless fobs — including KeeLoq HCS200/HCS300-based remotes and named Honda/Ford/GM/Chrysler keys — where a matching decoder exists. Unknown signals can be probed with the pulse analyser or the flexible `-X` decoder, recorded to file, or passed on for work in a tool such as [Universal Radio Hacker (URH)](universal-radio-hacker-urh.md).

## Where it fits

rtl_433 is the passive survey instrument of this section: leave it running to census what a vehicle (or a car park) is emitting — TPMS IDs alone are a tracking-relevant signal. The [KeyFob Analysis Toolkit (KAT)](keyfob-analysis-toolkit-kat.md) goes further on fobs specifically, with capture/decode/replay workflows rtl_433 deliberately lacks; URH takes over when no built-in decoder matches. A [Flipper Zero](flipper-zero.md) is the handheld equivalent for opportunistic capture, with transmit hardware rtl_433 does not use.

## Limits and safety notes

Receive-only cuts both ways: nothing here can replay, clone, or relay a fob — for the attack that forwards a genuine key's exchange see [key-fob relay attacks](concepts/key-fob-relay-attacks.md), which is a different mechanism entirely. Decoder coverage is uneven (roughly 380 decoders does not mean your sensor is one of them; some are disabled by default, and some need a specific frequency or sample rate noted in their README entry), and recognising a fob *transmission* is not decrypting a rolling-code *protocol* — the README makes no claim that KeeLoq or encrypted fob decoders recover keys. TPMS and fob identifiers are personal-data-adjacent in some jurisdictions; log them accordingly.
